Sellekni Business Model Canvas

A phone-based crash alert that reaches the emergency operations room by internet or plain SMS, with an estimated risk and a hospital suggestion. This canvas answers the workshop’s nine questions and marks what we have checked and what we still have to prove.

Version 31 Oct 2026Stage hackathon prototype, synthetic dataStatus hypotheses to validate before any price or partnership
Verified sourced fact or something we built and tested Hypothesis what we believe, not yet confirmed Next test how we will check it

The model in one sentence. Sellekni is a public-safety data layer that is free for the people who send alerts, and free for the emergency services that receive them during the pilot. Money, if any, comes from organisations that benefit from faster, better-structured crash reports (the State, insurers, fleets) and from non-dilutive funding. All of it is to be validated.

Block by block

The nine answers in detail

Each sheet starts with the workshop’s question, then gives our answer, how sure we are, and what would change our mind.

1

Customer segments

Who are your customers or beneficiaries?

Senders · free

Drivers and passengers with an Android phone. About 87 % of mobile OS share in Algeria is Android Verified · StatCounter

Witnesses at the scene, with a web link and no account.

Receivers · free in the pilot

Emergency operations rooms: Civil Protection 14 / 1021, Gendarmerie 1055, Police 17 / 1548 Verified, and SAMU where it operates (no national SAMU number confirmed).

Hospital emergency departments receiving a pre-alert.

Possible payers

The State, through the ministries that run the operations rooms (B2G). Motor insurers. Fleets and transport operators (buses, coaches, lorries). Employers with driving staff. Hypothesis

Why these payers

  • Motor insurance is the largest branch of the Algerian market: about 71.55 bn DZD, 41.6 % of 2024 premiums Verified · Trésor data via Atlas Magazine. Faster first notification could matter to them; we have not asked them yet.
  • The DNSR names heavy vehicles as a driver of the 2025 trend Verified · qualitative. Fleets already pay for tracking; crash notification could join it.
  • The receivers are public services, so “free during the pilot” avoids a procurement fight before anyone has seen value.

Next test Pilot early adopters: one wilaya’s Civil Protection ops room, one university hospital ED, and one fleet of professional drivers.

2

Value proposition

What problem do you solve for them?

Victim and passengers

“If I can’t call, my phone does, with my exact position, and I stay in control.” A 30 s alarm with an “I’m OK” button, then the alert sends itself. It works through plain SMS when mobile data is weak, and queues when there is no signal.

Witness

Report in 4 quick questions from a web link: no app, no account, no name.

Operations room

One merged, de-duplicated, located report with an explainable estimated risk and a hospital suggestion ranked by capability and travel time. The operator decides; every action is logged.

Hospital ED

A pre-alert with the mechanism and arrival estimate, which staff acknowledge, so the team is ready before the ambulance arrives.

Payers

Structured, time-stamped crash data and faster first notification Hypothesis

What we deliberately do not promise

  • We don’t replace 14 / 1021 or the regulating doctor: the app still opens the dialler.
  • We don’t make a diagnosis: transparent rules give an estimated risk.
  • We don’t claim real-time bed availability: that feed does not exist.
  • We don’t claim minutes or lives saved: the pilot measures it.

Why minutes matter, from the literature: automatic notification 1.2 min vs 5.7 min for witness calls in a US field test (NHTSA, 1990s, an analogy); each extra 10 min before hospital is linked to an odds ratio of 1.04 for in-hospital death (Gauss et al., JAMA Surg 2019, France).

3

Channels

How will you reach them?

Pilot

Android app installed directly (APK), the witness web link shared on the scene, and the dashboard in any browser on existing ops-room PCs.

After the pilot

Play Store. Its SMS permission is restricted, so we either follow Google’s policy route or fall back to opening the phone’s SMS composer pre-filled.

Distribution

Civil Protection awareness days, driving schools, university campuses, fleet onboarding, DNSR road-safety campaigns, and insurer welcome packs Hypothesis

Next test 8 drivers try the onboarding in 5 minutes; we record where they stop.

4

Customer relationships

How will you support and retain them?

Operators

Weekly co-design with a small group of operators, a one-day training (the web interface is standard), and a named contact at each pilot partner.

Drivers

A quiet app: no ads, no tracking outside an alert, Arabic and French with right-to-left screens. An SMS receipt tells the sender the alert was received.

Trust loop

Every alert can be closed as “false alarm” with a reason. Those reasons feed the detector tuning and are reported back to partners.

7

Key activities

What must you do?

  • Build and maintain the app, the gateway and the dashboard.
  • Collect real driving traces and retune the detector; measure false alarms per driving hour.
  • Verify hospital capabilities with each hospital, then keep them current.
  • Calibrate the risk score with emergency physicians on real cases.
  • Train operators and run the pilot measurements.
  • Privacy and legal compliance (law 18-07 as amended by law 25-11); legal review before any real deployment.
  • Security: per-device keys and a signed packet are next, because today’s check code catches corruption but is not authentication.

Cost structure

What will it cost?

Fixed

Team time (the main cost). Hosting: free tiers for the prototype, then paid hosting in Algeria at pilot scale. Map and routing hosting.

Per incident

SMS: roughly one acknowledgement to the sender and one pre-alert to the hospital per incident, plus the gateway SIMs.

One-off

Clinician and legal review, trademark and domain checks (INAPI), test phones. Hardware and homologation only if the in-car box on the roadmap goes ahead.

We have no quotes yet, so this canvas gives no figures. For the ops room: the dashboard runs in a browser on existing PCs, so there is nothing to buy during the pilot.

Self-assessment

Where the model is strong, and where it is weak

Strong

  • Senders pay nothing, which removes the adoption barrier.
  • Receivers are public services, so a free pilot is natural.
  • The data we produce (located, structured, time-stamped) is useful to several parties.
  • Non-dilutive funding routes exist and are verified.
  • No hardware to buy: existing phones, existing PCs, plain SMS.

Weak, and we say it first

  • No payer is identified yet.
  • Public buyers are slow.
  • The Play Store restricts the SMS permission.
  • Liability and privacy must be settled in writing before any real deployment.
  • An operations room has no reason to trust us yet; the pilot has to earn it.
  • Some recent iPhones and Pixels have built-in crash detection; Pixel detection and Android ELS were not available in Algeria at research time. We complement them.
